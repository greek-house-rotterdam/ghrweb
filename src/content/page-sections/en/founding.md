## From Asia Minor to the Netherlands

In the early 20th century, amid the political turmoil and ethnic cleansing following the collapse of the Ottoman Empire in 1922 and the displacement of indigenous Greek populations from Asia Minor, some refugees found their way to the Netherlands.

## Preparations and founding

These newcomers shared the same needs as the first Greeks who had settled in the Netherlands: to come together, preserve their identity, and build a church. However, times were difficult, and the growing threat of war in Europe delayed the realization of this dream.

Their wish finally came true after the end of World War II. In July 1946, the Association of Greeks in the Netherlands was founded. The Association was based in Rotterdam, which was quickly becoming the world's leading port.

## The first steps

During the first board meeting, Professor Dr. Sophia Antoniadis from Leiden University proposed the construction of a church, as the concept of a Greek community without a Greek Orthodox church was unthinkable. At the same time, it would serve the spiritual needs of the many Greek and other Orthodox sailors passing through the port.

After years of fundraising and thanks to a generous land donation from the Municipality of Rotterdam—located in what was then known as Museum Boymans van Beuningen Park—the construction of the St. Nicholas Greek Orthodox Church finally began.
