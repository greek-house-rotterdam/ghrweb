## From Asia Minor to the Netherlands

In the early 20th century, amidst the political turmoil following the collapse of the Ottoman Empire in 1922 and the displacement of Greek populations from Asia Minor, some of those displaced sought refuge in the Netherlands.

## Preparations and the founding

These displaced people shared the same needs as the first Greeks who settled in the Netherlands: to come together, preserve their identity, and build a church. However, times were difficult, and the growing threat of war in Europe delayed the realization of this dream.

Their wish finally came true after the end of World War II, in July 1946, with the founding of the Association of Greeks in the Netherlands. The Association was based in Rotterdam, which was quickly becoming the world's leading port.

## The first steps

During the first board meeting, Professor Dr. Sophia Antoniadis from Leiden University proposed building a church, as the concept of a Greek community was inseparable from a Greek Orthodox church. At the same time, it would serve the spiritual needs of the many Greek and other Orthodox seafarers passing through the port.

After years of fundraising efforts and thanks to a generous donation from the Municipality of Rotterdam—a plot of land in what was then called Museum Boymans van Beuningen Park—the construction of the St. Nicholas Greek Orthodox Church began.
