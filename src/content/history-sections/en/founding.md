## From Asia Minor and the Ottoman Empire to the Netherlands

In the early 20th century, amid the political turmoil and ethnic cleansing that followed the collapse and dissolution of the Ottoman Empire in 1922, and the subsequent displacement of Asia Minor’s thriving indigenous Greek population, a number of refugees found their way to the Netherlands.

## Preparations and founding

These displaced Greeks shared the same aspirations as the first Greek arrivals in the Netherlands: to come together, preserve their heritage, and build a church. But times were difficult, and the looming threat of war across Europe put those plans on hold.

Their wish finally came to life after the end of World War II, in July 1946, with the founding of the Association of Greeks in the Netherlands. The Association made its home in Rotterdam, a city that was rapidly growing into the world’s busiest port.

## The first steps

During the very first board meeting, Professor Dr. Sophia Antoniadis from Leiden University proposed building a church, noting that a Greek community without a Greek Orthodox church was simply unthinkable. It would also serve the spiritual needs of the many Greek and other Orthodox seafarers passing through the port.

Following years of dedicated fundraising, and thanks to a generous plot of land granted by the Municipality of Rotterdam in what was then known as Museum Boymans van Beuningen Park, the foundation was laid for the construction of the Saint Nicholas Greek Orthodox Church.
