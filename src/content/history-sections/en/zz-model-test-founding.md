## From Asia Minor to the Netherlands

In the early 20th century, amidst the political turmoil following the collapse of the Ottoman Empire in 1922 and the displacement of the Greek populations of Asia Minor, some of those uprooted sought refuge in the Netherlands.

## Preparations and the founding

These displaced Greeks shared the same fundamental needs as the earliest Greek arrivals in the Netherlands: to come together, preserve their heritage, and build a church. However, times were challenging, and the looming threat of war across Europe delayed the realization of this dream.

Their wish finally came true after the end of World War II, in July 1946, when the Association of Greeks in the Netherlands was officially founded. The Association was based in Rotterdam, a city that was rapidly growing into the world’s leading port.

## The first steps

During the very first board meeting, Professor Dr. Sofia Antoniadis from Leiden University proposed the erection of a church, pointing out that a Greek community was unimaginable without a Greek Orthodox church. At the same time, it would serve the spiritual needs of the many Greek and other Orthodox seafarers passing through the bustling port.

Following years of dedicated fundraising and thanks to the generous donation of a plot of land by the Municipality of Rotterdam—in what was then known as the Museum Boymans van Beuningen Park—the foundation was laid for the construction of the Church of Saint Nicholas.
